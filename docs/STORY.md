# Metoh — The Search

*Five friends, three nights, and the thing they came to prove is real.*

---

## The Expedition

Five friends. Three nights. One shared obsession most of the people in their lives had long since
stopped asking about.

They weren't scientists on a grant — not really. They were a cryptozoologist, a wildlife photographer,
a mountain guide, an audio engineer, and a field medic who had found each other the way believers do:
in the comment threads and the meetups and the long forum posts that stay alive at two in the morning.
What they had in common was the *metoh-kangmi* — the quiet, faintly embarrassing certainty that
something walks the high valleys above the treeline, and that everyone who mattered had simply stopped
looking.

The name is a joke history played on itself. A surveyor wrote down *metoh-kangmi* — near enough to
"man-bear snow-man" — and a newspaperman back in England mistranslated it into the *Abominable
Snowman*, and a hundred years of cartoons followed. The people who live under the mountain never
laughed. They called it what they had always called it and stayed out of its valley.

So they went looking. Together. Three nights above the treeline with everything they could carry and
one plain, impossible goal: find it, get it on record, and bring proof back down — the clean,
undeniable kind that a hundred years of blurry, arguable nothing had never managed.

## Arrival

> *This is the game's opening — a short first-person brief as you ride in, before the first night begins.*

The drive in is the last easy part. Tarmac gives way to gravel, gravel to a switchback track cut into
the valley wall, and the track itself gives up where the last village ends. Somewhere back there the
final bar of signal blinks out — you watch it go — and then it's the truck, the headlights, and a
wall of rock going copper, then grey, then black in the mirrors. They leave the truck at the last
teahouse. From here, everything is on foot, and everything is uphill.

For as long as anyone has written things down — and long before that, in the stories told and retold
over other fires — this valley has spoken back. Herders hear it before they ever see it: a cry that
belongs to nothing in the field guide, rolling down off the icefall in the dark. In the morning they
find the prints, too large and too deep and too far apart to laugh off, crossing a snowfield no one
crossed. And every so often, caught on a thorn at a height no bear could reach, a tuft of coarse pale
hair no lab has ever quite been able to name.

Centuries of it. And in all those centuries, not one person has brought home proof that couldn't be
explained away. That's the part that keeps them up at night — not that it might be real, but that it
has stayed hidden this well, this long, like it *chose* to.

Tonight the goal is simple, and it fits inside a lens: get it on camera. Real footage — steady, close,
lit, unmistakable — enough of it, pooled across the team and across the three nights, to end the
argument for good.

> *Filming is the fastest evidence path; snow casts and hair samples are the others. See
> `docs/CHARACTER_FUNC_DEV.md` and the roadmap.*

"Sundown in twenty minutes," said Wren, checking the sky the way only someone who has been genuinely
lost checks it — not for weather, for the time she can't get back. "After that, the valley belongs to
whatever lives in it."

They split the gear. They split the dark. They turned on their headlamps.

The wind came down off the glacier and took their tracks away behind them — everywhere but the deep
places, where the snow keeps what it is given.

## The Five Searchers

Each match, if you play a Searcher you're **randomly assigned one of the five** — so every night is a
different cut of the team and no two runs play the same. All five share the same core kit: move, sprint,
film, revive a downed teammate, dazzle the Yeti with a sustained light, and vault a fallen log. Their
**specialty** just makes each one better at a single thing.

> *Specialties are design intent — not all of them are wired up yet. See `docs/CHARACTER_FUNC_DEV.md`
> for what exists today, what's planned, and the implementation plan.*

### 🔬 Dr. Mara Okonkwo — *Lead Cryptozoologist*
Calm, exacting, the one who keeps the team from bolting. **Specialty: Analysis.** Collects and
casts evidence faster, and can confirm whether a find is "the real thing" — saving the team
from wasting the night on snow leopard scrapes and blue-sheep tracks.

### 📷 Eli Vance — *Wildlife Photographer*
Has photographed snow leopards at six feet and lived. Believes the lens is braver than he is.
**Specialty: The Photo.** His camera captures evidence at longer range and its flash can briefly
**stun and reveal** the Yeti — but it screams *here I am* down the whole valley.

### 🥾 Wren Castellano — *Mountain Guide & Tracker*
Knows this valley better than the maps do; lost a client to it once and never said so.
**Specialty: Tracking.** Sees fresh prints and broken crust glowing faintly in the dark,
moves quietly, and can mark the Yeti's trail for the team.

### 🎙️ Theo Park — *Audio Engineer & Podcaster*
Came for a season finale; staying for his life. **Specialty: Sound.** His parabolic mic hears
the Yeti's movement and breathing from far off, giving the team early warning — and a recording
counts as a full piece of evidence.

### 🩹 Sam Reyes — *Survivalist & Field Medic*
Ex‑search‑and‑rescue. Treats panic like a wound to be dressed. **Specialty: Endurance.** Revives
downed teammates faster, carries spare flashlight batteries to share, and keeps a cooler head
(more stamina) when the roaring starts.

## The Sixth Player — Yeti

Not a villain. A **resident**. It was here before the teahouse, before the switchback track, before the
survey that put a number on the peak and a mistranslation in a newspaper — here before the maps, before
the language the maps are written in. The stories the searchers grew up on, traded at midnight and
half-believed, are only the latest telling of stories the herders' grandparents heard, and theirs before
them: the tall thing, the quiet watcher at the edge of the light. Generation after generation has passed
the myth down without ever laying a hand on the truth of it. It has watched them do it.

It does not hate them; it does not think in the shape of hate. It simply does not want to be *found* — and
a hundred years and more of being looked for has taught it, with the patience of something that has all
the time in the world, exactly how the looking works. It knows light means people. It knows people in a
line means a search. It knows that if it waits in the dark long enough, the lights always, always come
apart.

And it knows the snow. It crosses the deep valleys at a stride while they flounder to the waist, and it
can read their whole night off the surface in the morning — every place they stopped, doubled back, or
split up, written where they could not help writing it. They came to track something. It has been
tracking them the entire time.

Tonight, for the first time, the lights might be smart enough to stay together.

---

## Three Nights

Each night runs from **8 p.m. to 8 a.m.** — twelve hours of dark to hunt in, or to survive. Three nights
running. The searchers win by banking enough clean footage before the third dawn; Yeti wins by living
to see it.

Dawn comes at eight. Not everyone out here is going to be there to watch it come up.
